# How do we know every claim names its supporting paper result?

The traceability check proves `13`'s claim-to-result pointing and serves `28`'s opponent criterion (`14`).

- **Claim:** `49` and the contribution claims (`116`).
- **Falsifier:** a claim presented as a contribution with no pointer to an appended-paper result, or a pointer to a label that no longer exists.
- **Check:** the acceptance criterion below is enforced by reading and by the build, not by a script.
- **Outcome:** passes for the current kappa; the four handovers and the premise are paired in `tab:taxonomy`, and the RQ answer cites the Summary of Papers and Synthesis chapters (`5`, `75`).
- **Residual risk:** the index is hand-kept, so nothing fails the build when a claim loses its pointer; the synthesis claim rests on the set, so it can weaken silently; the application is single-rater and used once, so it demonstrates a measure rather than validating it.

**Acceptance criterion:**

- Every contribution claim in the kappa names a paper result by pointer (`50`).
- Every gap names the handover where it occurs and the paper that closes it; a handover with no owning paper is a delimitation, not a gap (`84`).
- A claim with no pointer is not traceable and is not claimed as a contribution.
- A pointer to a label that no longer exists fails the build.
- The RQ answer cites the Summary of Papers and Synthesis chapters (`5`, `75`), never a single paper alone; the Paper IV synthesis claim points to its bounded case study (`4`).

**Owns:**

- The traceability acceptance criterion and its limits.
- That it proves `13`'s pointing obligation and serves `28`'s opponent criterion.

**Excludes:**

- `50` for the pointer convention and the current map; `49` for the claim wording.
- `84` for the derivation rule that names what a handover with no paper is.
- `43` to `74` for the evidence the pointers name; `14` for the requirement-to-check map.
