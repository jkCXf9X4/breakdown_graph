# What does a reviewer check about the kappa?

A reviewer reads the thesis with the three readers of `27` in mind and tests whether each one can get the argument from the kappa alone.

- **Criterion:** the examiner tests compliance with `29`-`28`; the opponent probes claim and evidence behind each clause; the doctoral continuation must be able to inherit the argument and the contract.
- **Needs:** the argument and contract in the kappa text, not reconstructed from the appended papers; each contribution names a paper result (`13`); every check in `14` passes or the gap is an explicit unproven obligation.
- **Failure:** a supportable claim left implicit fails all three readers; grading by severity is `120`.
- **Route:** a defect is fixed in the owning layer where the claim lives, never in this one; a repair that needs a project decision is an IMP in evolution.
- **Cross-level restatement:** a fact copied into a higher level than its owner — a paper result restated in a chapter node, a claim restated in a verification leaf — is a finding; route it to the owning leaf and replace the copy with an ID citation (`50`).
- **Method split:** a method fact that appears only in a chapter contract is a defect — the `MTH-*` node owns what was done, the `ARG-*` warrant why it is appropriate, the `VER-*` check whether it supports the claim.
- **Literature homes:** a grounded external fact that appears only in a chapter contract routes to `evidence/literature/` (`LIT-*`); its positioning routes to `argument/`; the citation placement stays in the section contract (`MS-*`).
- **Criteria update:** the three criteria here replace the 2026-11 run's findings leaves; that run is history under `32`.
