# Why are the design-research framing and the single case appropriate, and how is bias bounded?

The methodological argument answers why the method (`72`, `73`) is appropriate for the claim and how its threats are answered.

- **Warrant:** design research evaluates artifacts against the problem they solve, which fits a thesis whose evidence is artifacts and papers rather than hypothesis tests.
- **Binding order:** the append order I to IV is not the authoring order, and the chain was constructed after the results existed.
- **Binding mitigation:** a framework fitted to results already in hand is exposed to confirmation bias; the mitigation is that every chain step is tied to a result that existed before the chain did, and the retrospective application reports what the framework fails to recover.
- **Structural bias:** stated rather than mitigated, because the candidate is first author on all four appended papers, built the in-repo case, and is sole author of the kappa; each gap, premise, clause and delimitation is tied to a result and scoped to that result's evidence type.
- **Threat answer:** the single-case bound (`73`) rules out nothing beyond mechanism operation; claims of transferability are argued from construction, never demonstrated.
- **Section:** the argument is reported in the methods chapter (`93`).
- **Evidence:** `72`, `73`, `98`, `43` to `74`.
- **Literature:** `91` owns the validation/application-domain warrant, `92` the design-research framing.

**Owns:**

- The binding order, the confirmation-bias mitigation, and the structural bias statement.
- The warrant for the design-research framing and the single-case bound.

**Excludes:**

- `72` to `98` for what was done; `76` for what would falsify the claims.
- `43` to `74` for what each paper and the case establish.
- `93` for where the method and its threats are reported.
