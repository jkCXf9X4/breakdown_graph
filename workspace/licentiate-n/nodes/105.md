# Strip inline definitions

`thesis/custom/glossary.tex` holds 19 entries and is the definition authority.
`thesis/chapters/01_terminology.tex` prints that glossary and writes no definitions of its own.
The inline repeats are gone.
Those terms now resolve through the glossary labels (`\gls{gls-standardized-interface}`, `\gls{gls-complexity}`).
**Evidence:**

A term with two homes drifts, and the glossary and the prose can disagree.
A reader meets the same definition more than once and cannot tell which governs.
The terminology chapter sat at the end, so the reader met terms before definitions.

`01_terminology.tex` holds `\glsaddall`, `\printglossary`, and no prose.
`42` requires terminology aligned with the glossary.
**Acceptance:**

- Each glossary term is defined once, in `thesis/custom/glossary.tex`.
- Chapters reference terms with `\gls`-style commands only.
- No inline definition contradicts the glossary entry for the same term.
- Terminology material appears before the first chapter that uses a term.
**Outcome:**

- `01_terminology.tex` is the first included chapter file, ahead of `02_introduction.tex` (`thesis/thesis.tex:37`).
- "Terminology" and "Acronyms" print as unnumbered `\chapter*` front matter with `\addcontentsline` and `\markboth`.
- The narrative chapter numbers stay Introduction (1) to Author Contributions (8); the glossary consumes none.
- Cross-references in the introduction, background, and research-question chapters name the Terminology chapter instead of `\Cref{ch:terminology}`, because an unnumbered chapter has no number for `\cref` to print.
- Glossary descriptions no longer end in a period that the glossary style doubled.
- The build is green at 179 pages; the page total is unchanged because the glossary moved rather than grew.
**Out of scope:**

- Adding terms beyond those already used in the kappa.
- Acronym regeneration and the union table (`104`).
- Which section each term belongs to in the narrative.

- Glossary and acronym pipeline (`108`).
- Terminology consistency checks (`103`).
- Acronym regeneration (`104`).
